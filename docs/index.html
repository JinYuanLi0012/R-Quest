01 / THE PROBLEM

When the questions stop
being useful.

Our analysis identifies two recurring quality problems in self-generated questions: invalid questions and repeated variants of the same mathematical questions.

Answer disagreement can hide an invalid question.

Self-generated questions are not always well posed. Invalid questions become more prevalent across rounds, and answer-consistency filtering further increases their proportion in training data.

EXAMPLE FROM THE PAPER

Let x, y ∈ ℝ,
x + y = 3, xy = 3.
Find x2 + y2.

INVALIDNo real solution

R-Quest trains the solver to recognize and reject invalid questions before self-evolution.

Different wording.
The same mathematical task.

Generated questions can repeat the same mathematical task structure despite differences in wording, notation, or numerical parameters. Lexical similarity may not capture repetition at the level of question types.

59.5%of sampled round-five questions belong to the three largest question types.

The largest question type is split into 22 BLEU clusters. Such fragmentation can weaken the repetition penalty on a frequently repeated type.

02 / THE METHOD

Better questions.
Sustained self-evolution.

R-Quest introduces two forms of feedback: validity feedback from a solver initialized to recognize and reject invalid questions, and novelty feedback from the frozen base model.

01

Initialize for validity

Train the base model with GRPO to return a mathematical answer for a valid question or INVALID for an invalid one.

02

Assess task novelty

Use a frozen base model to compare sampled question pairs. A single same-type match is sufficient for rejection.

03

Co-evolve with feedback

These signals guide questioner rewards during alternating questioner–solver updates. The solver trains on the filtered questions.

SAMPLED PAIRWISE JUDGMENTS

Frequent task types
are less likely to pass.

We uniformly sample K other questions per candidate and reject the candidate if any reference is judged to be of the same type. This reduces the comparison cost from O(B2) to O(BK).

Pr(pass | p) ≈ (1 − p)K

Approximation when K is small relative to the batch size B.

Fraction of the remaining questions that share the same task type.

01 / COMPONENT ABLATIONS

Both signals matter.
Validity sustains the gains.

Either validity or novelty feedback alone achieves a higher peak score than R-Zero. However, validity feedback is more critical for sustained gains: its removal leads to late-stage collapse, whereas the variant without novelty feedback shows a milder decline and remains above the base model.
